Tokyo

17–19 Sep · Cogo Ryōgoku

Landing at 08:00 means day one is a real day, not a write-off. Ryōgoku sits on the JR Sōbu and Toei Ōedo lines, so almost everything below is one train or one easy transfer. Tap in and out with a Suica — no paper tickets needed. Just two nights here — Akihabara, teamLab, and Ueno are saved for the longer Tokyo stint at the end of the trip, so this stretch stays light.

Late-summer heat lingers: 26–31°C, humid, with an outside chance of a passing typhoon this time of year. Light breathable clothes, a compact umbrella, a refillable water bottle.

Day 1 · Thu 17 Sep — Asakusa & Skytree
Sensō-ji Templefree

Tokyo's oldest temple. Enter through Kaminarimon with its giant red lantern, walk Nakamise-dōri through the food and souvenir stalls, then the five-storey pagoda and main hall at the end. Busy by mid-morning — jet lag works in your favour here, go early.

Hakone

19–21 Sep · 2 nights, 1 full day

A mountain onsen town two hours from Tokyo. Two nights is the right length — enough for the loop and a proper soak, not so long that you run out of things to do. Staying at a guesthouse at 852-5 Yumoto, an 8–10 min walk from Hakone-Yumoto Station.

Cooler than Tokyo up in the hills: 18–24°C, with mist and drizzle not unusual. A light jacket earns its keep on the ropeway and after dark. This window overlaps Silver Week (19–23 Sep), Japan's first full 5-day holiday stretch since 2015 — expect bigger crowds and higher prices than a normal weekend.

Getting there¥2,500–3,500

Odakyū Romancecar from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto, reserved seats, about 85 minutes. Book the seat now — Silver Week Saturday demand is heavy. Aim for an 11:30 AM–12:00 PM departure from Shinjuku, giving buffer after checkout. Sit on the C or D side for Mount Fuji views. The Hakone Free Pass covers your loop transport once there, but the Romancecar itself needs its own reserved-seat ticket (¥1,200, or ¥1,150 as an e-ticket) on top of the pass.

From Cogo RyōgokuToei Ōedo Line direct to Shinjuku (~25 min, ¥280), then Romancecar (~85 min).
The check-in gapfree

Guesthouse check-in doesn't open until 4:00 PM, but the Romancecar lands you at Hakone-Yumoto around 1:00 PM — a ~3-hour gap. Stash bags in the station lockers and use the time to wander Yumoto's riverside shops and grab lunch, rather than rushing to the room.

Station to guesthouse8–10 min walk from Hakone-Yumoto Station, or a ~3 min taxi.
Open in Maps
The Hakone loopFree Pass ¥6,100

A circuit you do in one day using one pass: mountain railway to Gōra, cable car and ropeway over Ōwakudani's sulphur vents (buy a black egg, boiled in the hot springs), then a pirate-ship cruise across Lake Ashi with Fuji behind it if the weather holds, and a bus back. The Hakone Free Pass covers all of it.

StartHakone-Yumoto station, first departure of the day beats the coach tours.
Open in Maps
Onsen¥1,000–2,000 day use

The whole point of stopping here. Many ryokan open their baths to day visitors; if your lodging has its own, use it twice. Note that a number of traditional baths still refuse entry to visible tattoos — check before you go, or look for a private kashikiri bath you book by the hour.

Hakone Open-Air Museum¥1,600

Sculpture park in the mountains with a Picasso pavilion and a foot bath fed by a hot spring. Easy to fold into the loop — it's a stop on the mountain railway at Chōkoku-no-mori.

Kyoto

21–25 Sep · 4 nights

The longest single stop of the trip, and it needs to be. Kyoto's sights are spread across the edges of the city rather than clustered downtown, so group them by direction: east one day, west another, south another.

Still warm and humid most days, 25–30°C, easing off a little by evening. A lot of walking in the sun between temples — hat and sunscreen earn their keep.

Getting there~¥13,000

Bus or train down from Hakone to Odawara, then Tōkaidō Shinkansen straight to Kyoto — about 2 hours 15 from Odawara. Book this seat now: the Nozomi runs fully reserved-seat-only from Sep 18–23 for Silver Week — no unreserved cars at all that week, so this isn't a train you can just show up for.

Fushimi Inari Taishafree

Thousands of vermilion torii gates winding up a mountainside. The crowds thin dramatically after the first twenty minutes of climbing, and the full loop to the summit takes 2–3 hours. Open 24 hours — sunrise or after dark is when you get it to yourself.

From Kyoto StationJR Nara Line, 2 stops to Inari. ~5 min · ¥150
Open in Maps
Arashiyama & the bamboo grovefree · temple ¥500

Western edge of the city. The bamboo path is short and extremely busy after 9am — go at opening. While you're out there: Tenryū-ji's garden, the Iwatayama monkey park up the hill, and the Hozu river.

From Kyoto StationJR Sagano/San-in Line to Saga-Arashiyama. ~17 min · ¥240
Open in Maps
Higashiyama: Kiyomizu-dera & Gion¥500

Do this as one long walk. Kiyomizu-dera's wooden terrace over the hillside, then downhill through the preserved lanes of Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, through Yasaka Shrine and into Gion for the evening. This is where you're most likely to see geiko and maiko heading to appointments around dusk — watch, don't chase.

Kinkaku-ji¥500

The gold pavilion over a reflecting pond. It's a fifteen-minute visit on a fixed one-way path and it is always crowded, but the image is the reason people come to Kyoto. Pair it with Ryōan-ji's rock garden nearby to make the trip north worthwhile.

From Kyoto StationCity bus 101 or 205. ~40 min · ¥230

Free-roaming deer that bow for crackers, and Tōdai-ji, which holds a 15-metre bronze Buddha in the largest wooden building you'll ever stand in. Half a day is enough, and it's the best use of your fourth Kyoto day.

Osaka

25–28 Sep · 3 nights, 2 full days

Loud, unpretentious, and built around eating. After four days of temples this is the palate cleanser. Only 15 minutes from Kyoto by Shinkansen, or half an hour on a regular train.

Similar to Kyoto — humid days around 25–29°C. Evenings by the canal cool down enough for a light layer.

Dōtonborifood only

Canal-side strip under the Glico running man sign — takoyaki, okonomiyaki, kushikatsu (never double-dip the sauce). Best after dark when the signs are lit. Adjoining Hōzenji Yokochō is a quiet stone alley one street over if you need a break from it.

From Osaka StationMidōsuji subway to Namba. ~8 min · ¥240
Open in Maps
Osaka Castle¥600

Reconstructed keep in a large moated park. The interior is a museum with a lift, so it's more history exhibit than castle — the grounds and the view from the top floor are the draw.

From Osaka StationJR Osaka Loop Line to Osakajō-kōen. ~11 min · ¥180
Open in Maps
Kuromon Ichiba Marketfood only

Covered market of seafood, fruit and grilled skewers, much of it cooked in front of you. Go hungry and mid-morning; stalls start closing in the late afternoon.

Hiroshima

28 Sep–1 Oct · 3 nights, 2 full days

Two full days so you don't have to choose between the city and Miyajima. Give the Peace Park a morning at least — it deserves more time than people usually plan for.

Milder and breezier by the coast, 22–27°C. The Miyajima ferry crossing can be windy — a light windbreaker helps.

Getting there~¥10,500

Sanyō Shinkansen from Shin-Osaka to Hiroshima, about 90 minutes on a Nozomi.

Peace Memorial Park & Museum¥200

The A-Bomb Dome, the cenotaph, the children's memorial hung with paper cranes, and a museum that is deliberately difficult. Allow three hours and don't schedule something demanding straight afterwards. The museum entry is almost free by design.

From Hiroshima StationTram line 2 or 6 to Genbaku Dome-mae. ~15 min · ¥240
Open in Maps
Miyajima & the floating torii~¥1,000 + ¥300 shrine

Island in the bay where the great torii stands in the water at high tide and you can walk out to it at low tide — check the tide table before you pick your hour. Itsukushima Shrine sits on stilts over the same water, wild deer wander the streets, and the ropeway up Mt Misen gives you the inland sea. A full day.

From Hiroshima StationJR Sanyō Line to Miyajimaguchi (~27 min, ¥420), then the JR ferry across (~10 min, ¥200). Plus a ¥100 island visitor tax.
Open in Maps
Okonomiyaki, Hiroshima style¥900–1,400

Layered rather than mixed, with noodles inside — a different dish from the Osaka version and worth eating both to compare. Okonomimura is a building of stacked okonomiyaki counters near the Peace Park.

Tokyo again

1–6 Oct · 5 nights, 4 full days

Back on the Shinkansen — about four hours direct from Hiroshima. This stretch grew to 5 nights after the first Tokyo stint got trimmed to dodge Silver Week lodging costs, so Akihabara, teamLab, and Ueno — originally Day 3–4 of the first stint — live here now, alongside everything else. The 6th is a travel day only.

Autumn starts to show: 19–25°C, cooler mornings and evenings. Good excuse for the vintage-jacket shopping in Shimokitazawa.

Day 1 · Thu 1 Oct — Akihabara & teamLab
Akihabara Electric Townfree to browse

Multi-floor anime, manga, retro games and electronics. Super Potato for old consoles, Mandarake for second-hand everything, Yodobashi Camera if you want the enormous mainstream version. Arcades here are free to enter and cheap to play.
